# Project name ideas

Date: 2026-09-06
Status: Brainstorming; no name selected and no project rename performed.

## Project premise and naming direction

The current project is a village simulation about values, relationships, cooperation, conflict, and different ideas about living well. It explores tradeoffs without declaring one philosophy the winner.

The expanded naming direction considers a whole world, separate shards, branching worlds, and multiple worlds meeting or colliding. These are possible future themes, not claims about implemented features. A broader project name could contain individually named villages and worlds, preserving the original shortlist as setting names.

## Original shortlist

The initial recommendation was **Arcadia**, especially **Arcadia: Ways of Living**. A place name suits a community whose people must discover how to live together.

| Name | Inspiration | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arcadia | The literary ideal of a harmonious pastoral land. | An inviting village name with a question beneath it: can these people actually build a good life together? |
| Hestia | Greek goddess of the hearth, home, and communal fire. | Warm and memorable; suits relationships, shared resources, and surviving the first winter. |
| Mimir | A Norse figure associated with wisdom and the well of knowledge. | Emphasizes observation, memory, and learning from the village's history. |
| Baucis | One of the humble hosts in the legend of Baucis and Philemon. | An unusual choice rooted in everyday kindness and the moral significance of ordinary people. |

Original title variants:

- **Arcadia: Ways of Living**: strongest overall connection to the original premise.
- **Hestia: A Village of Choices**: warmest and most approachable.
- **Mimir's Vale**: a newly coined place name with a more mythical atmosphere.

Arcadia suggests an ideal; the simulation lets the villagers discover how difficult that ideal is to sustain.

Sources: [Arcadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia_%28utopia%29), [Hestia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hestia), [Mimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%ADmir), and [Baucis and Philemon commentary, University of Virginia](https://ovid.lib.virginia.edu/sandys/8comm.htm).

## Mythological names for a larger cosmology

These cultural references are established; their proposed application to this project is creative interpretation.

| Name | Reference | Project interpretation | Naming tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yggdrasil | The Norse world tree connecting the worlds. | One living structure supports many societies and branching histories. | Strongest direct mythological fit for multiple worlds, but spelling is demanding. |
| Bifrost | The bridge between Midgard and Asgard. | The crossings between worlds bring different values into contact. | Emphasizes connections and travel more than the societies themselves. |
| Ginnungagap | The primordial gap where heat and cold meet in the Norse creation account. | New worlds and ways of living emerge from the meeting of opposing forces. | Particularly apt for collision and creation, but difficult to say and remember. |
| Janus | Roman god of doorways and beginnings, represented with two faces. | Thresholds between worlds, alternative futures, and the consequences of crossing. | Short and accessible, though it sounds more like a figure than a world. |
| Indra's Net | A Buddhist image of jewels reflecting one another. | Each world is distinct yet reflects and influences all the others. | A philosophical metaphor from a religious tradition, rather than a legendary place. |

Sources: [Swedish History Museum on the world tree](https://historiska.se/en/explore-history/history-hub/the-mythological-world-of-the-vikings/), [Bifrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifr%C3%B6st), [University of Texas account of Norse creation](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/doherty/plan2/amycreation.html), [American University of Beirut Libraries on Janus](https://online-exhibit.aub.edu.lb/exhibits/show/aub-main-gate/gates-and-their-significance/a-university-gate), and [Indra's Net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra%27s_net).

## Evocative titles for worlds and shards

These are creative title suggestions, not assertions of mythological origin or commercial availability.

| Name | What it suggests | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confluence | Separate streams meeting and changing one another. | Worlds and philosophies encounter one another through both cooperation and conflict. |
| Shardweave | Distinct fragments woven into a larger whole. | Multiple shards retain their identities while forming relationships. |
| Worldweft | Worlds as threads in a shared fabric. | Interwoven histories and consequences; poetic and expansive. |
| The Manyfold | Many worlds folded into a shared reality. | Parallel societies, branches, and layers; an intentional wordplay on manifold. |
| Worldfall | The arrival or collapse of worlds. | Dramatic collisions, upheaval, and survival. |
| The Meeting Worlds | Worlds encountering one another. | Clear, thoughtful, and compatible with peaceful or hostile contact. |
| The Between | A place at the boundaries of realities. | A shared meeting ground where societies negotiate coexistence. |
| Shardwake | The aftermath of a fracture, or fragments awakening. | Emergent life and societies after worlds break apart. |
| Mosaic Worlds | Distinct pieces forming a larger picture. | Pluralism and contrasting ways of living without requiring catastrophe. |
| The World Orchard | Many worlds growing from a shared ground. | A warmer, organic cosmology that preserves the village's intimate tone. |
| A Thousand Hearths | Everyday lives spread across many communities. | Large scope with a human center. |
| The Unfinished World | A reality continually shaped by its inhabitants. | A philosophical simulation with no predetermined ideal society. |

## Hybrid titles

The following combine mythological references with invented title language:

- **Yggdrasil: Worlds in the Balance**
- **Bifrost: Worlds in Contact**
- **Janus: Divergent Worlds**
- **Arcadia: A World in Fragments**
- **Shards of Arcadia**
- **The Arcadian Confluence**
- **Hestia: A Thousand Worlds**

“Shards of Arcadia” implies a fragmented ideal or broken world. It is especially strong if the narrative begins with fragmentation; “Confluence” leaves room for worlds that arose independently.

## Recommended shortlist for the expanded direction

1. **Confluence**: best overall thematic fit. Both worlds and worldviews meet, and the outcome remains open.
2. **Shardweave**: best if shards become the central organizing idea. Suggests relationship and construction as well as fracture.
3. **Yggdrasil**: strongest direct mythological name for a connected collection of worlds.
4. **The Manyfold**: best for branching realities and contrasting possible lives.
5. **Shards of Arcadia**: best continuation of the original recommendation, with a more dramatic premise.

Possible naming structure: **Confluence** is the project; **Arcadia** is the first world; **Mimir's Vale** is its first village; **First Winter** is the opening season. This is a proposal, not a change to the current setting.

Suggested tagline: **Many worlds. Different ideals. Shared consequences.**
